# Flora art plan — 33 species

For the current user-reviewed species pass, see [flora visual review log](../flora_visual_review_log.md). This older plan remains the broader art backlog.

This plan treats the [photographic reference board](flora_reference_board.md) as structural grounding. Each species keeps its fictional palette and ecological role. Reference photos are study material, not texture assets. Build mature, juvenile, and stressed variants from the same branching rule; vary age, asymmetry, loss, and lean through stable seeds.

## Shared art rules

- At habitat distance, each species must read from its silhouette before color: tree forks, crown mass, grass or reed profile, and colony edge.
- At inspection distance, show how each part connects: trunk to scaffold to twig to leaf; runner to rooted node; stem to petiole; fungus to substrate. Avoid isolated floating triangles, flat discs, and identical radial repetition.
- Color within a species should describe age, exposure, and tissue: darker sheltered bases, restrained warm or pale new growth, occasional stressed tips. Preserve color contrast across species under neutral light and canopy shadow.
- Use physically plausible roughness and subtle normal variation at the right scale. A bark groove or leaf vein should not look like a terrain boulder; wax, wetness, and matte cuticle need distinct responses. Hand-author or procedurally generate original textures; license any external asset before import.
- Keep geometry where silhouette changes. Use inexpensive material detail for bark pores, fine venation, spore texture, and moss nap. Avoid transparent foliage stacks that grow fill cost. Use reference-scene captures and camera-motion profiling before raising instance counts.
- Acceptance for each species: one solitary mature form, a juvenile, a stressed form, a clustered/habitat view, and a neutral-light close-up. Compare 12 seeds for distinct but recognizable silhouettes, triangle count, bounds, normals, and frame cost.

## Build order and checkpoints

1. **Readability first:** finish tree crown structures and vine/reed/groundcover distinctions. Test at the standard habitat camera where silhouette matters most.
2. **Ecological surfaces:** moss, lichen, fungi, and slime mold need substrate contact and convincing patch boundaries before microscopic texture.
3. **Materials:** add species-specific bark, cuticle, wax, wetness, underside, and age cues using original compact maps or low-cost shader inputs. Keep transparent overdraw bounded.
4. **Variation:** tune seeded growth for juvenile/mature/stressed sets and grouped scenes, while avoiding identical copies and maintaining species identity.
5. **Validation:** run deterministic and finite-mesh tests, record triangle counts and reference captures, compare visual quality and camera movement against the baseline. Treat the performance agent's renderer edits as an integration boundary; merge after both branches settle.
