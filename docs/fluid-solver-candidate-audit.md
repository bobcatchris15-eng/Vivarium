## Remaining blockers before production integration

1. **There is no domain-boundary outlet, so water cannot leave the graph.** Graph construction adds only internal +X and +Z neighbor edges (`WaterVolumeGraph.cs:39-58`); there are no exterior boundary edges. `BoundaryOutflow` is stored and included in snapshots (`95-116`) but never incremented by the solver. The bowl foundation assertion explicitly expects `BoundaryOutflow == 0` (`FluidSolverFoundationTests.cs:45-46`). This is conservative as a closed graph, but it cannot implement island-edge drainage or account for outflow after downhill water reaches the domain boundary. If domain drainage is intentionally out of scope for this candidate, document that boundary condition; if production acceptance includes water leaving the island, add boundary flux accounting before integration.

2. **The candidate is not wired into production.** A repository search for `WaterVolumeGraph`, `WaterVolumeSolver`, `WaterVolumeState`, and `WaterVolumeSource` in solver-worktree `src` found only their definitions; constructors and `Advance` are called only from `FluidSolverFoundationTests.cs`. `VivariumWorld` and existing `Hydrology` still own production water. The passing foundation tests establish solver-local behavior, not player-visible fluid behavior or interaction with tools, world persistence, or render surfaces.

## Physics and timing evidence

- The graph creates one square-area node per in-domain grid cell (`WaterVolumeGraph.cs:27-35`) and only +X/+Z adjacency edges with sill equal to the higher adjacent bed (`39-58`). This forms a connected orthogonal support graph; edge width and distance are each one `CellSize`.
- The local-inertial edge update uses free surface relative to sill, `depth = max(0,max(headA,headB))`, downhill head difference, and drag (`WaterVolumeGraph.cs:225-242`). It sums outgoing flux per source node and scales flux to available volume before applying deltas (`244-268`), which is the principal nonnegative-volume/conservation guard.
- The initial audit's fixed-step/source-history finding was resolved in the candidate during review. Current `Advance` queues a cloned, ordered source schedule with its duration in `PendingInputs` (`151-168`); each substep consumes that schedule in order (`195-217`). Snapshots include pending inputs (`WaterVolumeState.Export/Restore`, `100-112`). A focused test advances 140 seconds, turns the source off while backlog remains, then verifies the original 140 seconds of inflow is still accounted and backlog drains (`FluidSolverFoundationTests.cs:101-120`).
- The solver now caps maximum step at 0.25 s, uses CFL factor 0.45, and caps work at 512 steps per call (`WaterVolumeGraph.cs:122-126`). `StableStep` uses local depth, edge velocity, wave speed, and edge distance (`170-193`). That permits up to 128 seconds per call at the maximum step; high flow can reduce throughput and accrue backlog. Pending input preserves correctness across that backlog, but actual scheduler cadence and worst-case sustained high-flow throughput still need measurement before integration. Production hydrology cadence is 2 fixed ticks (20 simulated seconds; `VivariumWorld.cs:65,159`).
- Existing probes cover rising a bowl pool and internal sill spill at actual source rate, downhill motion from rest, mass conservation, and snapshot continuation (`FluidSolverFoundationTests.cs:11-120`). The latest focused suite passed 12/12.
- No dedicated equilibrium/hydrostatic-rest assertion or spatial-resolution/step-size sweep was found. Positivity scaling and the CFL formula do not by themselves demonstrate non-oscillatory equilibrium across supported depths and resolutions. These are validation gaps, not reproduced failures.
- `Sill = max(adjacent cell beds)` is a cell-to-cell sill. Narrow ridges and sub-cell saddle geometry cannot be represented more finely than the terrain support grid; acceptance should include sills near grid resolution and both sides of the sill tolerance.
- Full `CellSize²` area is assigned even at clipped boundary supports (`29-35`). This is internally mass-conserving for the graph but the effective island footprint is the union of full cells whose centers lie in the hex, rather than an exact clipped hex. Confirm that approximation is acceptable for volume and shoreline behavior.

## Review conclusion

The candidate demonstrates low-rate hollow filling, internal sill spill, downhill motion, internal volume conservation, and source-history-preserving backlog in its focused tests. Before integration, define and test the domain boundary condition/outflow behavior, add hydrostatic equilibrium and resolution/stability coverage, and verify sustained throughput under the actual scheduler cadence and high-flow cases. Keep the candidate's status as a numerical foundation until it has a production caller and end-to-end save/render/input validation.
