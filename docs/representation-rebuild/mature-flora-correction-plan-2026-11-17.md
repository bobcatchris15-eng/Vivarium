# Mature flora correction plan

Based on the November 17 fitted mature review. Compare the front, side and elevated views of the same seeded specimen. Use close views, host surfaces, water, coverage tiles or night lighting where the original image could not answer the question. Accepted species silhouettes and ecological behavior guide every correction.

## Plumestep grass (plumegrass)

**Finding.** The seedhead is an evenly stepped rainbow cone on an exceptionally thin, straight stalk; its colored structures read as repeated tiers more than loose feathery material. The basal blades form a fine radial burst.

**Correction and check.** Stagger and narrow the colored feather barbs and strengthen the stalk while retaining the rainbow gradient.

## Bannerreed (bannerreed)

**Finding.** Broad leaves form a compact, regularly layered mound while the flower stalks emerge as long, mostly bare parallel rods; the abrupt change in scale and sparse attachment along the stalk make the assembly feel staged.

**Correction and check.** Carry alternating curling leaves farther up each culm so the nodding panicle connects visually to its foliage.

## Coalspike (coalspike)

**Finding.** The straight, very thin central stem rises from a strongly radial tuft and carries a smooth, capsule-like dark head. The head-to-stem transition and any stalk sheathing are too small to verify in these views.

**Correction and check.** Preserve the single dark spike, strengthen the load-bearing stem, align the head and vary the basal straps.

## Banksorrow (banksorrow)

**Finding.** The curtain makes a coherent silhouette, but the crown appears as an almost even dome and the trunk exits the ground like a cut cylinder. Branch-to-curtain attachment and leaf detail need close inspection before drawing stronger conclusions.

**Correction and check.** Break the dome with uneven primary scaffold heights; extend the bole into tapered buttresses and keep hanging twigs continuous at their attachment.

## Hollowcrown (hollowcrown)

**Finding.** The roots form several sharp, similarly tapered spurs and the main limbs depart in a conspicuously radial cluster. The distinctive trunk cavity is not visible in these three angles, so its rim and branch union require a targeted view.

**Correction and check.** Stagger upper limbs, soften repeated root spurs and inspect the cavity from its actual opening before reshaping its rim.

## Witchberry (witchberry)

**Finding.** The twisting trunk is distinctive, but the side view exposes a large smooth loop and little visible root flare; the foliage resolves into a few compact pads. The trunk surface and limb joins need a close view.

**Correction and check.** Keep the contorted trunk, tighten the smooth loop into asymmetric wood growth, and open up the fan-leaf sprays around irregular burls.

## Antlerlace lichen (antlerlace_lichen)

**Finding.** The upright branches radiate from a dark central star and rise at conspicuously regular intervals; the pale basal mat has a hard, circular paper-cut edge. Fine thallus texture and branch joins need a closer view.

**Correction and check.** Retain branching upright thalli; conceal organizing axes and spread overlapping basal lobes into an uneven margin.

## Ashcomb (ashcomb)

**Finding.** Caps repeat the same stacked horizontal bands and pointed cone silhouette; the thin, nearly straight stems and abrupt cap-to-stem connection give the group a manufactured look. Natural honeycomb relief is not clear at this framing.

**Correction and check.** Vary cap profiles and stipe inclinations, carry the stipe into the cap, and inspect recessed honeycomb relief from the side.

## Ashfingers lichen (ashfingers_lichen)

**Finding.** The erect thalli look like a ring of thin green rods on a pale disc; the overhead view exposes a spoke-like central arrangement and a sharp mat edge. Finer lichen tissue is difficult to judge at this scale.

**Correction and check.** Preserve upright fingers in small irregular groups and replace the spoke-and-disc appearance beneath them with overlapping thallus.

## Ashwire (ashwire)

**Finding.** The basal foliage converges into a highly regular spiky radial tuft, while several tall seed stems are similarly straight and spaced. The overall grass habit reads, but the repeated geometry is visible.

**Correction and check.** Group fine blades into off-center, uneven tillers and vary culm lean. Inspect gaps between tufts.

## Barbarrow (barbarrow)

**Finding.** The large, sharply triangular leaves form a very tidy cup around a bundle of thin, straight petioles. The few flower tiers appear evenly spaced and nearly horizontal, giving the plant a constructed profile.

**Correction and check.** Vary spear-leaf petiole lengths, angles and flower-node spacing while keeping the accepted leaf and flower identities.

## Barrowbole (barrowbole)

**Finding.** The three caps are smooth, similarly shaped domes with thick simple cylindrical stalks; limited surface variation and abrupt joins make them read as assembled primitives at this view.

**Correction and check.** Break repeated cap domes with age-dependent outlines and broaden upper stalks into offset cap attachments.

## Bitterbine (bitterbine)

**Finding.** The overhead silhouette resolves into a conspicuously simple three-pronged runner with uniformly packed leaves; the low side view does not expose enough stem attachment detail to judge rooting or support.

**Correction and check.** Bend and branch runners asymmetrically and vary leaf density at their nodes. Check host-supported growth separately.

## Blackneedle (blackneedle)

**Finding.** The tightly symmetrical basal rosette and very thin, straight central stalk read as a separate ornament on a base. The intended leaf spines and head anatomy are too small to verify reliably here.

**Correction and check.** Break the symmetric rosette, taper the central stalk into its head and inspect the black leaf spines at macro scale.

## Blue Sundew Mat (blue_sundew)

**Finding.** The mat reads as a very regular circular rosette with a cluster of similar smooth blue knobs; the defining fine trapping structures are not visible at this distance, so the organism risks reading as a decorative succulent.
