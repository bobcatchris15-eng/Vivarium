# Surface liquid rebuild and night lighting correction

Implemented directly in the shared checkout October 10–11, 2026. No Clanker dispatch. This document supersedes the implementation status in `surface-fluid-and-night-flora-handoff.md`; the earlier documents and failed probes remain historical evidence.

The subsequent 5 ml resolution refinement, mirrored low-rim pool checks, current live-clock evidence and save compatibility details are in [surface-liquid-resolution.md](surface-liquid-resolution.md). Validation below describes the initial rebuild; use the newer document and its `artifacts/fluid-resolution/` receipts for the refined solver.

## What now owns water

The normal game uses `SurfaceLiquid` and `LiquidDynamics`. Every parcel carries an actual cubic-metre volume, a three-dimensional position and velocity. Springs add discharge to this state at a fixed physical step of 1/60 second. Sub-parcel source volume is retained. Gravity, position-based density constraints, solid boundary support, a small cohesion term and XSPH viscosity determine movement. There is no procedural turbulence or vorticity injection. The source issues a downward, coherent jet from a small hollow outlet.

The old spring tracer lifetime and the terrain depth-field solver do not advance or render game water. Both public legacy hydrology advance paths return when the liquid owner is attached. New game creation skips the old depth-field warmup. Resting parcels never expire, and solver load never deletes excess water. A hollow can retain water and build pressure until water overtops its lip. Gravity moves the retained volume along the downward slope.

The solid boundary supplies the analytic integral of the Poly6 kernel over a half-space. This corrects a substantial error in the first rebuild: the bed had been treated as missing neighbours and allowed too much liquid to pack into the floor. The constrained volume now rises and spills in the enclosed hollow fixture.

## What is removed from visible rendering

`WaterSurfaceRenderer` takes immutable three-dimensional liquid parcels as its only geometry input. `LiquidSurfaceMesh` reconstructs a closed surface using velocity-aligned, determinant-preserving kernels and a sparse surface net. Its short-lived 3D sampling lattice is a mesh construction detail. It contains neither terrain elevation nor ecological wetness/depth cells, and it is discarded after each mesh build. The mesh can exist in the air, form a vertical jet, make a deep pool and overflow a sill.

The normal game does not draw `SpringWaterMesh`, the previous ground ribbons, tracer-density surfaces or wet-cell depth sheets. The terrain renderer writes zero to the former dynamic water-head texture channel; the terrain shader cannot manufacture a second visible surface from the ecology grid. That grid remains a derived habitat query only. The explicit hydrology debugging overlay still offers diagnostic cells when requested.

`WaterRenderer` builds the standing water-table pond independently. It refreshes when terrain or the configured water level changes. Moving liquid transfers into that reservoir only when its centre physically reaches the exposed table surface over ground below that surface. Low ground alone cannot teleport fluid into the pond. The transfer, boundary departures, tool removal, evaporation and infiltration have separate budget entries.

`liquid.gdshader` uses reconstructed normals, depth-aware screen refraction, Fresnel reflection and modest freshwater absorption. It does not add cosmetic foam, wave motion, water displacement or turbulence. The pond retains its own standing-water appearance.

## Clock, background work and persistence

Physical water runs on elapsed real time multiplied by the user's playback speed. It is independent of the accelerated biological/calendar clock. Pause stops new emission and queues no further physical time. A dedicated worker advances the solver against a terrain/prop snapshot; the main thread publishes completed state and budget deltas together. Queued time is retained when a job is busy. A separate worker constructs and prepares the render mesh, keeping the density solve and geometry construction off the frame thread.

Saves use water format 3 for the liquid state: parcel positions, velocities, volumes and source IDs, source remainders/emission phases, physical time and remaining queued time. Save capture finishes pending physics before taking the water budget and derived ecology samples. Existing format 2 depth saves are converted once to the equivalent parcel volume when the game attaches the liquid owner. Old save serialization omits the new null liquid property, preserving their digest verification. The user's original autosave was read through an isolated copy and was not overwritten by review runs.

Debug/editor builds now enable optimization in both the simulation and game assemblies. The unoptimized render/update loops had made initial captures misleadingly slow. The rebuild also replaces the first, expensive marching-tetrahedron surface with a smaller surface net and prepares mesh upload arrays on its worker.

## Bioluminescence

Every visible luminous plant owns a stable light keyed by its organism ID. The rotating nearest-32 pool is removed. New lights receive the current night envelope immediately, and all registered lights receive the same continuous night factor. Forward+ clustered element capacity is 4096. Light energies and ranges are deliberately gentle; neighbouring emitters share a bounded local intensity through overlap normalization, with none taking a turn or being switched off to make room for another.

Emission masks: ghostbonnet downward-facing gills only; lanternsedge seed heads only; siltstars broad middle stripe on each leaf; tidebell a narrow strip around the entire leaf perimeter, including the tip/base. Glowbriar retains its distinct arching canes and underside nodules. All seven species cast coloured light on the surrounding terrain and neighbouring objects at night. Tissue emission strength was reduced along with cast-light intensity. The close-up pass also fixes bonnet LODs to retain complete caps/gills/stipes and corrects Siltstars' vertical scale so their low leaves remain visible above the silty floor. Gill backfaces cannot leak glow through the cap top.

## Research basis

The density solve follows the position-based fluids approach of [Macklin and Müller, 2013](https://mmacklin.com/pbf_sig_preprint.pdf). Gentle cohesion/viscosity and persistent particle volume also draw on [Clavet et al., particle-based viscoelastic fluid simulation](https://diglib.eg.org/items/7cad7994-b781-40ce-ad04-0bf61dc94279). Velocity-aligned surface reconstruction is informed by [NVIDIA's fluid surface anisotropy documentation](https://nvidiagameworks.github.io/FleX/1.2/ue4_docs/FLEXUe4_FluidSurfaceRendering.html). The surface-net topology is informed by [Frisken Gibson's surface-net research](https://www.merl.com/publications/TR99-24); this implementation places vertices at averaged scalar crossings rather than implementing that paper's binary-data elastic relaxation. Screen-space smoothing was reviewed through [Narrow-Range Filter](https://www.cemyuksel.com/research/papers/narrowrangefilter.pdf), but no such depth-filter pipeline is implemented here. These are practical real-time methods, not a claim of a fully resolved CFD solver.
